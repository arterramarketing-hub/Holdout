  HOLDOUT — single-file browser game

  TUNING VALUES — every number lives in a table below: CFG, WEAPONS, SIGHTS, FALLOFF, ETYPES, COVER_KINDS, TOWN,
  SPAWN_ZONES, TERRITORIES, KILLSTREAKS, MOODS.

  CAMERA — real 3D (three.js r186). First person is the default (see CONTROLS). The third-person chase cam sits
  4.2 m behind and 2.0 m above the controlled soldier's right shoulder, looking 5 m ahead; a desktop window taller
  than it is wide pulls back to 5.6 m / 2.6 m / FOV 72. In both views you steer the camera with your aim, and
  movement is camera-relative. Red arrows mark close enemies beside or behind you. The chase camera pulls in front
  of house walls rather than clipping through.

  GRAPHICS QUALITY — SETTINGS › Graphics: Auto (the default), Low, Medium or High (QUALITY). LINES sets the N64
  render's height (High 480, Medium 360, Low 270; the whole-number blow-up never goes much past it, so 720p renders
  360 and 1080p 540 on High); DPR caps the output pixel ratio (1 / 1.5 / 2); caps thin out what is drawn and kept
  (particles 220 / 500 / 900, loose brass 40 / 100 / 160, enemy corpses 50 / 90 / 120, rain 35 / 70 / 100%); Low
  also drops the tracer glow and other soldiers' muzzle blooms (your own flash always blooms). Auto starts at High on
  every device (the look phones already ran), steps down after 3 s of frames averaging over 24 ms (and again if it
  stays slow), and climbs back after 10 s under 17.5 ms, never within 30 s of a drop. Stalls over 0.2 s (a throttled
  or hidden page) are ignored.

  CONTROLLER — the standard Gamepad mapping. Battle: left stick moves (radial deadzone 0.15), right stick looks
  (deadzone 0.12, response curve ^1.8, SETTINGS › Controller look), RT fires, LT aims down the sights, RB throws a
  grenade, X reloads, Y switches view, L3 sprints until the stick comes back, D-pad ← ↑ → calls killstreaks 1-3, START
  opens settings, VIEW goes back to the map. Menus: the stick or D-pad moves focus (an orange ring), A presses, B backs
  out (and closes settings); on the start menu LB/RB (or ←/→) cycle sectors, A deploys and Y opens the loadout.
  Rumble follows the Vibration setting. The HUD hint shows controller buttons whenever the controller was the last
  thing touched, and a key hands the prompts back. Aim assist stays third person only.

  BASIC TRAINING — the firing range, run up Main Street from the FOB gate (RANGE, TRAIN_STEPS): move to a marker,
  look at three signs, knock down three pop-up steel targets at 10 m, two at 22 m down the sights, reload, sprint to
  the square, frag a cluster of three, and take a training flag (a 4 m ring, neutral, quicker to take). Each step's
  card says what to do in the words of the input you last used — keyboard and mouse, touch, or controller — with
  progress dots, a check when it is done, and SKIP. Targets ring and fold when hit and never die (no kills, no
  killstreaks); there are no enemy waves, no reserves, no Warlord, nothing can hurt you, and nothing is saved to a
  sector. A new campaign is offered it on first launch (Skip remembers the answer; a save from before the range
  never asks), and the range button beside the start menu's settings opens it any time. The finish card deploys to
  the selected sector or goes back to the map; ◂ MAP leaves it at any point.

  ORIENTATION — on a phone or tablet the game plays in landscape only: held upright it shows a rotate
  prompt and the simulation stops dead until you turn it, so nothing happens while you cannot see it.

  CONTROLS — you aim and you fire; there is no auto-fire and no lock-on (saves that used them load aiming by hand,
  in the view they had). First person is the default. The mouse aims (click to capture the pointer, Esc frees it),
  left mouse fires, right mouse aims down sights; on a phone the right side of the screen looks, a FIRE button
  shoots (drag off it and you keep aiming) and ADS toggles sights. SETTINGS opens from the start menu's cog, the
  cog in battle, or O. Aim assist (on by default, third person only — first person is yours to aim) pulls the
  crosshair toward a target already under it and slows the look 35% while it is there; it never fires for you.
  The crosshair is white, turns RED the moment an enemy is lined up under it, and blooms while you move, fire or
  reload; hits punch a four-tick marker across it (white for a hit, red for a kill, longer for a headshot).
  G throws a grenade and Shift sprints (see GRENADES and SPRINT). Down the sights your cone shrinks to a quarter of the hip cone (a fifth or
  less through the ACOG or scope), the view zooms by the sight's magnification, and you move at 70% speed.
  V switches between third and first person: first person puts the camera at the soldier's eyes with a weapon
  viewmodel (bob, sway, recoil kick, the full reload, muzzle bloom, the weapon pulled in against walls); the
  death camera still plays in third person. Switching apps, a notification or a lost touch releases every held
  input, so the view never keeps turning on its own.

  HUD EXTRAS — a minimap (top-left) draws the town, your squad, pickups and any enemy within 24 m or
  firing within 40 m, with your facing as an arrow; damage-direction arcs point back at whatever hit you;
  a kill feed lists kills and losses; short vibrations on hits, kills and your own death where the browser
  supports them. All of those, plus squad callouts, look sensitivity, field of view (-10 to +18), sound, music,
  graphics quality and (once a controller has been used) controller look speed, are settings.

  DEATH — when your soldier falls you watch it happen: a 1.9 s letterboxed death camera, the world slowed to 30%
  for most of it, easing around and down to the body with the mix muffled behind a pulse; then a reinforcement
  puts you back on the line. Bodies take 1.6 s to drop (enemies 1.1 s) in three beats — hit jolt, knees,
  gravity — falling backward when shot from the front and forward when shot in the back, and the weapon lands
  on the ground beside them and stays there.

  REINFORCEMENTS — you are one soldier and you stay that soldier. Die and you watch it happen, then you come back
  at a reinforcement spawn as the death camera ends; squadmates walk back on by themselves after CFG.respawn
  (3 s). The squad shares one pool of CFG.tickets (20) and every death on your side spends one, yours and theirs
  alike. Once the pool is empty, squadmates who fall stay down and your own next death loses the front (so does
  losing all four). No body-swapping.

  KILLSTREAKS — supports are earned, not bought: 4 kills without dying gives INCENDIARY, 7 ARTILLERY,
  10 SUPPLY DROP (KILLSTREAKS table). Dying resets the count. Nothing is purchased anywhere in the game:
  there is no currency, no armory, no investments, no commendations, and nothing progresses while you are
  away — the map is a level select and the only thing it tracks is which territories you hold. Holding one
  grants nothing but the ground: there are no passive bonuses.

  FOUR AGAINST EIGHT — you and three squadmates against up to eight enemies on the field at once. The enemy has its
  own ticket pool (CFG.enemyTickets 44 + 8 per tier): every enemy death spends one, a new enemy walks on 1-2 s after
  one falls while tickets remain, and the front is won when the pool is spent (the progress bar is exactly that).
  The Warlord arrives at 90%, and killing it breaks what is left of them. Objectives spend their pool too (see
  OBJECTIVES). A front you walk away from resumes where you left it; a lost front starts again from 0%.

  OBJECTIVES — every sector is fought over three of six landmarks (OBJ_SITES: Rail yard, Market, Church square, Gas
  station, Building site, Cemetery; SECTOR_OBJ picks each sector's three), lettered A-C north to south, each a flag
  in a 7 m ring, all starting in enemy hands. Only one side standing in a ring moves it: one soldier takes an
  objective in 12 s, two in 9 s, three or more in 7 s (neutral halfway); both sides in the ring freeze it. Taking one
  spends two enemy reserves at once. Holding more objectives than the enemy drains a reserve every 4 s (every 2 s
  with all three), never one still standing on the field and never the Warlord's own ticket, so the front is still
  won by fighting. Holding fewer costs your squad a reinforcement every 20 s once the front is 45 s old. About 45%
  of riflemen and gunners work objectives (take the nearest they don't hold, rush back to one under attack, hold
  from cover inside the ring) and the enemy reinforces near objectives it holds, never into one being taken. Your
  POINT pushes the nearest objective you don't hold and takes it from cover inside the ring; OVERWATCH covers it
  from range; FLANK keeps hunting. The HUD shows A-C under the ticket bar (owner colour, a bar for a capture in
  progress, a pulse when contested) with a one-line message when one changes hands; the minimap draws each ring
  and letter; in the world each has a flag that climbs its pole as it is taken, a dashed ring on the ground, and a
  letter tag over it that shows through fog and walls. The start menu's briefing names the sector's three.

  SPAWN ZONES — no front line. Fifteen zones sit in the open ground of the streets and yards (SPAWN_ZONES), and
  the FOB is your squad's alone. An enemy walks on at a zone far from your squad and preferably out of its sight,
  never within ~16 m of it and rarely the zone used a moment ago; your reinforcements come back far from the
  enemy and near a teammate, favouring the FOB. Only the opening is a standoff: you start at the FOB.

  SQUAD ROLES — your three do not trail you. Each fights its own nearest enemy in a fixed role: POINT closes
  to 60% of its range, FLANK swings wide to one side, OVERWATCH holds at the edge of its range. Spots are
  pushed apart so nobody stands within ~8 m of a teammate or of you. With nothing on the field they sweep
  toward the last gunfire.

  WEAPONS — UMP45 (SMG) / M4A1 (AR) / M249 SAW (LMG) / Intervention (sniper) / Rocket, stats in the
  WEAPONS table and fixed: there are no weapon levels and no weapon XP, so a rifle is the same rifle on
  the first front and the last.
  ATTACHMENTS (BATTLE PREP; saved per weapon in meta.attach; your own rifle only, squadmates carry the
  stock gun): a SIGHT — iron sights (1.45x), a 1.2x red dot, a 4x ACOG, and on the Intervention its own
  6x scope in place of irons — and a MAGAZINE, standard or extended (UMP45 32/48, M4A1 30/45, SAW
  100/200, Intervention 5/8; an extended mag adds 10% to the reload). Zoom is tan-based (the 4x shows a
  quarter of the view), look sensitivity scales down with it, the ACOG and scope shoulder slower than a
  dot, and a magnified optic tightens ADS spread a little more. Every sight is real geometry on the
  model (GUN_BUILD): at full ADS the viewmodel is placed so the rear aperture and the front post — or
  the optic's eyepiece — sit exactly on the camera axis at a short eye relief (adsInfo), and the
  crosshair steps aside. Magnified optics add a DOM window (#sight) whose radius is computed from the
  eyepiece ring's real size at that relief, with the chevron or crosshair drawn on top; the red dot is a
  DOM dot glowing inside its 3D housing. The muzzle flash and tracers start at the model's own barrel
  tip (GUN_MUZ). Nothing changes squad damage: no upgrades, no perks. Sniper pierces up to 2 targets.
  Rockets explode (r70, no friendly fire from your own rockets). AI squadmates fire 2.6x slower than the
  controlled soldier.

  DAMAGE — riflemen and gunners 3 HP, runners 2, raiders 4, breachers 9; M4A1 1.05, UMP45 0.85, SAW 1.0,
  Intervention 4, rocket 3 (blast). So a rifleman takes 3 M4 rounds, 4 from the UMP45 up close and 5 at
  15 m; a headshot doubles a round. Enemy health does not grow with tier (that broke the count): each tier
  adds 12% to enemy damage instead. BATTLE PREP shows the hits it takes to drop a rifleman.

  DROP-OFF — a round does full damage out to the weapon's `near` distance, then falls in a straight
  line to a floor by the time it has flown its whole reach: SMG full to 10 m then 60% by 22 m, AR full
  to 20 m then 60% by 37 m, LMG full to 16 m then 55% by 35 m, pistol 50% by 20 m. The sniper and the
  rocket have none. Measured on the distance the round actually travelled, so it stacks with cover and
  with a headshot rather than replacing them. Squad fire only; enemy rounds do not drop off.

  YOUR ROUNDS GO TO THE CROSSHAIR — in either view, a shot is a ray through the town: it flies in 3D to the first thing under the crosshair (an enemy, a wall, cover, the ground) with the
  weapon's spread around that line, so aiming up and down matters. Buildings and cover have real heights
  (COVER_TOP), riflemen are shorter while they kneel (bodyTop), and your headshots are decided by where the
  round strikes (the top fifth of the body, near its middle). Enemy rounds are 3D too (ENEMY FIRE); squadmates keep
  level fire and the probabilistic cover rule below.

  HEADSHOTS (squadmates) — their rounds fly level, so a target is a circle and its
  head is the narrow column at the middle of it: a round whose line passes within ~0.1 m of the target's centre
  (0.15 m on a breacher, 0.22 m on the Warlord) can take the head for CFG.headshotMul (2x) damage. Measured
  perpendicular to the shot, so it tracks where the round was aimed. Being in that column is necessary but not
  sufficient: a level round has no height, so the rest is rolled at CFG.headshotChance (35%). Explosions never
  headshot, and enemy rounds have no headshot, so nothing can one-shot your soldiers out of nowhere. A headshot
  gives a sharper hitmarker (gold), a harder buzz, a higher spray and an HS tag in the kill feed.

  AMMO — magazines are finite: UMP45 32, M4A1 30, SAW 100, Intervention 5, Rocket 1 (extended mags
  48 / 45 / 200 / 8), with 2-6 spare mags each.
  Reloads (1.7-4.2 s) play out in full — mag out, hand to the pouch, fresh mag in, charging handle —
  and start automatically when the mag runs dry, on R or a tap of the ammo counter, or in any lull once
  the mag is under half. Out of spares, everyone falls back to a bottomless pistol. Enemy dead drop a
  magazine 35% of the time (one mag, 30 s on the ground); care packages refill the whole squad.

  HAZARDS — mines (8/front, r55 blast, 2 dmg, triggered by either side) and fire patches from incendiary
  strikes, which burn BOTH sides (enemies 1.6/s, soldiers 0.8/s). Your artillery also has friendly-fire
  (3 enemy / 1.5 soldier dmg). There is no enemy bombardment: no mortars, no spotters calling them in, and
  no distant artillery in the ambience.

  ENEMIES (ETYPES) — rifleman 3 HP (330 range, 2–3 round bursts of 0.5 dmg), runner 2 HP / 1 dmg melee (wave 2+),
  breacher 9 HP / 2 dmg behind a riot shield that soaks 70% of frontal fire (wave 3+), ATV raider 4 HP / 2 dmg
  (tier 2+ or wave 5+), PKM gunner 3 HP / 1 dmg (tier 2+ or wave 2+: 380 range, 4–7 round bursts). Each tier adds
  12% damage and 4% speed; health never scales. The radio spotter still has a model but is not spawned.

  BOSS — at 90% front progress a WARLORD spawns (60 HP +35%/tier, HUD health bar) and holds progress at 99% until
  killed. Phases by HP: above 66% it fights in melee; from 66% to 33% it also summons up to 3 riflemen every 8 s,
  never taking the field past eight besides itself; under 33% it enrages (+45% speed, a 10-round radial burst
  every 5 s). Killing it wins the front.

  TOWN & COVER — Valmont, a fixed 96 x 72 m mountain town built out of places (TOWN): the RAIL YARD (station,
  platform, freight wagons on two tracks, water tower), the MILL YARD (warehouse, shipping containers, log
  piles), the CHURCH SQUARE (church and 19 m bell tower, fountain, war memorial, café tables), the MARKET
  (stalls, a produce van), the GAS STATION (four pumps that explode, a shop, a fuel tanker that makes a very
  big bang), the CEMETERY (chapel, gravestones, hedges, trees), the BUILDING SITE (a bare frame on twelve
  columns under a crane, gravel mounds, a mixer, a site fence), the FOB (HESCO with gates, a sandbag emplacement
  open at its corners, tents) and the MOTOR POOL (trucks), strung along Main Street (a burnt-out bus in it), a
  cross street and a south road. Buildings, wagons and containers are solid with real heights; fixed cover is
  always there, and 55 slots roll fresh cover each battle. A wall down each side, a fence and closed gate across the
  pass road, and HESCO to the south stand exactly on the edge of play, so you can walk up to them.
  Every 3D round — yours and the enemy's — meets cover as geometry: it stops on anything taller than its line at
  that point (COVER_TOP), passes over anything lower, and low-block cover (fences, hedges, tents) still rolls its
  stop chance. Your squadmates' level rounds keep the old rule: cover stops 35-95% of them (per kind), and an enemy
  hugging cover or a wall (within 18 px of its edge) takes 35% less. Cover takes the damage of what it stops;
  blasts damage all cover in range. Melee ignores cover.

  ENEMY FIRE — enemy rounds fly in 3D: from the shooter's shoulder (80% of its height, so a kneeling rifleman fires
  low) at the target's chest (62% of its height), with the old side-to-side spread and half as much up and down.
  A standing soldier is 1.8 m tall; an AI squadmate settled behind cover crouches to 1.1 m, so low cover protects
  them, while you (who never crouch) are only covered by what is taller than the line to your chest: a car or a
  van stops nearly everything (a round over the roof can still find your head), sandbags stop what comes in low.
  In the open, damage per minute stays within 15% of the level-round game (tests/baseline.json). A sprinting
  soldier draws 40% wider fire. The Warlord's radial burst flies level at 1.2 m. No enemy round is a headshot.

  GRENADES — two frags each life, refilled by a care package. G (desktop) or the grenade button (touch) throws one
  from the eyes along the crosshair, 12° above it, at 15 m/s; it falls under gravity, skips off the ground, walls and
  the tops of cover, and bursts 2.2 s after it leaves the hand (CFG.frag*). The blast reaches 5 m, from 6 damage at
  the centre to nothing at the edge: a rifleman dies within ~2.5 m. Walls of buildings shield it, a breacher's shield
  does not, it chews cover, it never hurts your squad, and it hurts you at half strength — never enough to kill you
  from full health. Throwing drops a sprint; the gun dips out of the way for the throw. A red grenade on a ring
  around the crosshair points at any live frag within 6 m. The ammo block shows how many you carry; on a phone the
  grenade button's badge does.

  SPRINT — hold Shift, or on a phone push the move stick out to its rim and forward (within ±35°) for a beat; an
  orange chevron over the stick shows it. 1.45x speed, forward only (within ±50° of where you look; it keeps going
  down to 80% of the stick and ±50°), no stamina limit. No firing and no sights while sprinting: the trigger or ADS
  drops the sprint, and the gun takes 0.18 s to come up before the first round. Reloads carry on. The view widens
  by 8° and the gun swings low across your chest.

  TACTICS — everyone paths around buildings and cover on a 1 m A* grid. Riflemen and PKM gunners bound to the free
  cover nearest a point at 70% / 85% of their range (offset toward a flank), hold 2.6–5 s firing bursts, get
  pinned when rounds crack within 1.5 m (suppression slows them and spoils their aim), and fall back when under
  45% HP. Runners flank 13 m wide down side streets, and breachers come straight in behind their shields. Your
  own soldier goes exactly where you move it: nothing latches onto cover or crouches for you.

  NO DIALOGUE — there is no radio chatter, no comm box and no personalities. Soldiers keep names (the kill feed
  uses them on desktop) and nothing else talks. The one story beat left is CPL Dana Wren joining the squad when
  you take territory 7 in the first theater.

  TOUCH CONTROLS — Call of Duty Mobile's layout and proportions (both triggers marked with a round, sights
  marked ADS): left thumb moves, right thumb looks, and
  the trigger is duplicated on both sides so you can fire with whichever thumb is free. Flat translucent
  circles sized off the screen height (--fb = 22vh, clamped 66-108 px) so they keep CoD Mobile's ratios on
  every phone: fire 1, left trigger .8, ADS .68, grenade .62, reload .6. The fire button sits up off the
  corner under the right thumb, ADS above-right of it, reload up and to its left, the grenade low beside it
  (with a count badge), the left trigger above the left thumb, and the corner is left clear for the ammo count.
  Reload outlines red under a quarter mag. Pushing the move stick out to its rim sprints.

  MAP — 13 hand-authored territories in a Risk-style adjacency graph; attack any enemy territory next to one you
  hold. Taking one flips it to you, and that is all it does. Taking them all starts a new theater: the map resets
  (HQ kept) and every enemy tier rises by 2.

  N64 LOOK — three.js loads from jsdelivr at boot (the map works before it arrives; DEPLOY waits).
  Output is N64-faithful: the scene renders at about 480 lines on High (the N64 hi-res mode; see GRAPHICS QUALITY)
  on the short side into a render target, then a full-screen pass at native resolution quantises to RGB555 with a 4x4 ordered
  dither and blows each scene pixel up by a whole number with nearest sampling — crisp, never smeared.
  Characters are rigid limbs on joint hierarchies (the N64 technique) built from rounded lathe-turned
  limbs, torsos and joint caps — a modern (2019-era) war: infantry in multicam with plate carriers,
  mag pouches, knee pads and ballistic helmets with NVG mounts and ear pro (roster colour on IR and
  shoulder patches; carriers alternate coyote and ranger green), carbines with optics, suppressed
  SMGs, belt-fed LMGs, anti-materiel sniper rifles and RPGs, against a paramilitary force in
  balaclavas, goggles and chest rigs carrying AKs and PKMs. All textures are hand-painted on canvases
  at 4x and shrunk to 64 px cells; every piece carries baked vertex shading. The battlefield is
  Valmont (see TOWN & COVER) under snow-capped ranges and painted clouds. Every character and settled
  corpse draws through shared instance batches. ?fps=1 shows fps / draw calls.

  ENEMY MODELS & SET PIECES — every enemy type has its own model (knit-cap knife runner, breacher with riot shield
  and sledgehammer, kneeling PKM gunner, radio spotter with binoculars, machete raider on a quad bike, and the
  Warlord as a bomb-suit juggernaut with a minigun whose armour and visor heat up in later phases). Warlord
  arrival: 1.9 s letterboxed hero shot + OoT-style title card with an epithet, world at 30%. Cover pieces look
  battered below half health and leave rubble when destroyed. Shells visibly fall for their last 0.35 s, the
  incendiary strike brings a jet pass, care packages parachute in. Fallen soldiers get a battlefield cross (rifle,
  helmet, boots). Damaged squadmates and 3+ HP enemies show floating health bars.

  DAY/NIGHT & WEATHER — each battle rolls a time of day (dawn 15%, day 40%, dusk 25%, night 20%; the
  campaign's very first battle is always dawn) that drifts forward one step every 150 s, and weather
  (clear / overcast / fog / rain; rain and fog get 4% likelier per tier, capped at +20%). Sight =
  time-of-day visibility (dawn 90%, day 100%, dusk 88%, night 78%) × weather (overcast 95%, fog 80%,
  rain 85%), floored at 70%. Sight scales squad weapon range, AI engage radius and enemy gunner range equally.
  Rain adds streaks, a hiss layer, and lightning with thunder. The start menu's briefing forecasts each attackable
  sector's sky (FORECAST, rolled when the sector is first shown) and that is the sky you deploy into; fighting
  there uses the forecast up, so the next visit brings new weather.

  START MENU — a field operations table: the theater map on the left, the mission briefing on the right, nothing to
  scroll. The thirteen sectors are regions split by distance with hand-wandered borders, drawn over contour lines
  rising to the northern ranges, a river past Velen Crossing into Dunmoor Marsh (with marsh symbols) and a lettered
  grid; held ground is washed blue and hatched, theirs faintly red, and an orange FRONT LINE glows and marches
  wherever the two meet. Markers are NATO-style: a blue square for held (a star at HQ), an orange diamond with a
  pulse for attackable, a dim red diamond for out of reach, a double diamond for the capital, and a progress ring
  for a sector partly taken. Routes: blue supply lines behind the front, orange arrows along each axis of advance.
  A reticle locks onto the selection; a tap anywhere in a region selects it; ←/→ cycle the attackable sectors and
  Enter deploys. The briefing: theater progress in 13 segments with the firing range and settings buttons, the sector's status chip, number and grid
  reference, its name, THREAT (tier and pips), RESERVES (what is left of the enemy pool), FORECAST, a progress bar
  when partly taken, one line of orders naming the sector's objectives (or which sectors to take first), DEPLOY (RESUME when partly taken) naming
  the sector, your kit, and the squad of four with roles. Opening the map plays its entrance (land, sectors, the
  front drawing itself, markers popping out from HQ, the briefing sliding in; off under reduced motion), a sector
  just taken flashes, and the selection moves to the next fight beside it. A desktop window taller than it is wide
  puts the briefing under the map.

  MENUS — LOADOUT is the five guns in a rail on the left and the
  one you carry on the right: stat bars (damage, fire rate, range, magazine, mobility, reload), sight and
  magazine choices, DONE. A desktop window taller than it is wide stacks the columns.

  UI — flat and modern, in the Battlefield manner, built on the brand sheet: near-black navy (#05080f),
  hairline slate rules, one orange accent (#ff6b2c), square corners, and only the call to action glows.
  Archivo Expanded for headlines and buttons, Archivo for reading, JetBrains Mono for labels and numbers
  (Google Fonts; system fallbacks offline). The HUD is a two-sided TICKET BAR top-centre (your
  reinforcements in blue, the enemy's in red, your kills between), the minimap top-left, the menu
  buttons top-right, and the weapon block and killstreak slots bottom-right. Menus are an eyebrow with a
  rule, an expanded headline, and hairline panels; the start menu is the operations table above. Every end-of-front screen is the after-action layout: eyebrow,
  headline, one line, a hairline stat grid (kills, accuracy, headshots, longest kill, reinforcements,
  time), a solid orange button and a mono note. The rotate prompt uses the same system.

  MUSIC MOODS — the battle cue has six moods (MOODS), picked from the sky and re-picked as it drifts: DAY (D minor,
  104 BPM, the driving cue), DAWN (E dorian, 96, lifting plucks and a bell), DUSK (A minor, 98, a dotted figure
  with its echo), NIGHT (C minor, 88, drone, sparse pings, deep slow drums), RAIN (D minor, 92, trembling pad,
  falling plucks) and FOG (F sharp minor, 84, slow swells, drone and bell). All layer in with the fight's heat.

  MUSIC — written in code like everything else and played live on the audio clock (updateMusic, musStep):
  a MENU cue (D minor, 72 BPM: detuned pad walking i–VI–III–VII, sub bass, a slow heartbeat, a plucked
  arpeggio, a bell at phrase starts) and a BATTLE cue (D minor, 104 BPM, i–VI–VII–V then i–bII–VII–i: bass
  pulse, dark pad and hats always; string ostinato, taiko with a backbeat rim and fills, and brass stabs
  layer in with the heat of the fight — enemies near and close, rounds flying, you hurt, the Warlord — with
  a braam per phrase at full tilt). Stingers for a sector taken, a front lost and the Warlord's arrival.
  Cues cross-fade on screen changes; the MUSIC slider in settings sets the level (0 is off); M mutes it and
  the death cam muffles it with everything else.

  SOUND — no sample files: at the first tap every weapon sound is synthesised offline into 32 kHz
  stereo buffers (sub-millisecond attack, saturated muzzle blast, a falling pressure thump, early
  reflections at 70–620 ms, a rolling outdoor tail, the action cycling), 1–3 variants per weapon plus
  a distant variant, rendered in 10 ms slices. Playback adds ±3% pitch, distance low-pass and stereo
  placement around the camera. Squad carbines/SMGs/LMGs/.50 sniper/RPG, enemy AKs and PKMs, supersonic
  near-miss snaps (within 1.75 m), ricochets, brass casings, layered explosions with
  debris, body falls, a strike-jet flyby and outgoing artillery. Ambience: wind, a low front rumble
